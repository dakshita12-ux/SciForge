Title: Support Vector Machines

Support Vector Machines (SVMs) are supervised learning algorithms used
for classification and regression.

A support vector classifier tries to find a decision boundary that
separates classes while maximizing the margin between them.

Support vectors are the training observations closest to the decision
boundary. They help determine the position of the boundary.

A linear kernel is useful when a linear decision boundary is appropriate.
Nonlinear kernels, such as the radial basis function (RBF) kernel,
can model more complex decision boundaries.

The parameter C controls the trade-off between margin width and
training errors. A smaller C allows more violations of the margin,
while a larger C penalizes training errors more strongly.

SVMs can be sensitive to feature scales. Standardization is often
important, and the scaler should be fitted only on training data.

SVM models can be evaluated using suitable metrics such as accuracy,
precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrices for classification.
For regression, metrics such as MAE and RMSE may be appropriate.
